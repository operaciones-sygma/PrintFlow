## Anti-Patterns Verdict

NOT AI slop. This reads as a hand-built, domain-specific tool: real workflow logic, idiosyncratic-but-purposeful choices, the team's own vocabulary. A user fluent in Linear/Notion would trust the *logic*; they'd pause at the *density and the inline-style drift*, not at "AI made this." v10.65.1 already removed the side-stripe-border ban (moved to full tinted borders) — good.

Deterministic scan (detect.mjs): 8 warnings.
- overused-font ×2: Geist (screen, L1403) — acceptable for product but generic; Arial (L2179) — the PRINT template, correct for paper, false positive.
- layout-transition ×6: `transition: width/height` on progress bars / animated widths (L1634, 5711, 7449, 9738, 10292, 14805) — animates layout props → jank. Use `transform: scaleX` / `grid-template-rows`.

No live browser overlay (no browser-automation available in this environment).

## Overall Impression

It works, the team trusts it, and it speaks their language better than almost any off-the-shelf tool would. The drag is restraint: the board card tries to say everything at once, and 15k lines of inline styles have drifted into a dozen near-identical-but-not button/badge treatments. The single biggest opportunity: make the OCard whisper the routine and shout the exception.

## What's Working

1. **Domain fluency (heuristic 2 = 4/4).** Roles, folios, stages, maquila economics, the Spanish + emoji register — it mirrors how this shop actually talks and works. That's the hardest thing to fake and it's genuinely excellent.
2. **Status & error-prevention density.** Toasts, busy overlays, live timers, REIMPRIMIR, falta-costo, folio immutability, stock locks, confirm dialogs. The app rarely leaves the user guessing or lets them shoot their foot.
3. **Contextual guidance.** GuideBanner (per-stage/role hints) and the wakeup flow teach the interface in-context instead of a manual nobody reads.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] OCard badge wall.** The board card can stack ~15 small colored pills (stage, web, stock, priority, #prod, retraso, stale, falta-costo, prueba, file, plate, created_by, sin-logo…). For Gerardo glancing at a tablet, the one thing he needs ("what do I make next") competes equally with fiscal/price/meta badges.
- Why it matters: the board's whole job is fast triage; equal-weight badges defeat it.
- Fix: tier them. 1–2 primary status badges stay; collapse the rest into a quiet "+N" / overflow, or push meta (created_by, file, plate) to the detail. Reserve red/amber for true exceptions.
- Command: `/impeccable distill` then `/impeccable layout`

**[P1] Consistency drift from inline-style sprawl.** Buttons, badges and fontSizes are hand-styled per-call, so the same intent renders slightly differently across screens. The edit-button-only-on-the-card bug (just fixed) is a symptom: affordances aren't defined once.
- Why it matters: each tiny divergence is a micro-pause; it's also why bugs hide.
- Fix: extract a tiny vocabulary — `Badge`, `Button` variants, a fixed type scale — from the existing `bt`/`bs`/`C` tokens.
- Command: `/impeccable extract` then `/impeccable audit`

**[P2] Color over-allocation.** Many accent hues (ios, fac, cart, emp, sal, maq, amb, dn, ctp, dsn…) appear across badges and stages, so saturation no longer signals priority.
- Why it matters: when everything is colored, color stops meaning "look here."
- Fix: split a tighter palette into roles — status vs category vs action — and keep full saturation for active/primary/exception only.
- Command: `/impeccable colorize` (or `/impeccable quieter`)

**[P2] Layout-property motion.** Progress bars animate `width`/`height` (6 hits) → layout thrash on a board that re-renders often.
- Why it matters: jank on the busiest screen, on shop tablets.
- Fix: `transform: scaleX(...)` for bars, `grid-template-rows` for height reveals.
- Command: `/impeccable optimize`

**[P2] Pure-white surfaces + ad hoc type scale.** `bg`/`card` are `#ffffff` (no tint) and fontSizes run 9–22 with many in-between values.
- Why it matters: untinted white reads slightly clinical; a loose scale weakens hierarchy.
- Fix: tint neutrals toward the slate brand hue (chroma ~0.005–0.01); define a 5–6 step type scale.
- Command: `/impeccable typeset` + `/impeccable colorize`

## Persona Red Flags (real users)

**Lupita (secretaria — high-volume capture, desktop).** Captures most orders all day. No command palette or keyboard-first capture; the long OrderForm + the badge wall to triage cap her speed. She's the power user the efficiency heuristic (2/4) is failing.

**Genaro (vendedor — phone / field).** Dense OCard on a small screen; until today the maquila cost edit was unreachable from the detail modal (the path he actually uses); 9–10px text + many badges are rough on mobile.

**Gerardo (producción — shop-floor glance).** Needs one answer ("next physical action") from arm's length on a tablet. The badge wall + many colors + small text mean the primary signal doesn't dominate; he has to parse, not glance.

## Minor Observations

- Geist is fine for product UI but generic; a more distinctive face would add identity if you ever want it.
- Heavy emoji in timeline/print is charming and on-brand for this team, but it coexists with a Phosphor icon system elsewhere — pick the line.
- Several 9px labels are borderline for readability at tablet distance.
- Modal-heavy, but v10.72.14 (flow buttons inside the detail modal) was a good de-modal move — keep pushing actions inline.

## Questions to Consider

- What if the card showed one primary status and a quiet "+N" for the rest?
- Does production need fiscal/price badges at all, or only the next physical action?
- What would a "calm at arm's length" version of the board look like on the shop tablet?
